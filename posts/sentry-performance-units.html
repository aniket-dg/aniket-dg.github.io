Our Sentry bill was quietly tracing things nobody would ever debug

Sentry makes it trivial to trace everything — every request, job, query, re-render. That's exactly the problem: the parts of the bill that grew fastest were the ones nobody was ever going to look at.

Originally posted on LinkedIn ↗

The problem

Sentry billing crept up until it was a real line item, and the instinct was to assume it was tracking something important — more traffic, more errors, more users. It wasn't. The biggest contributors to Performance Units were infrastructure talking to itself: health checks, job-scheduler heartbeats, and metrics-scraping routes, all faithfully traced end to end, all producing close to zero errors, none of them anything a human was ever going to open.

The actual problem

Sentry makes it trivial to trace everything — every request, job, query, re-render. Easy to add isn't the same as worth paying for. Nobody had been selective about what actually warranted monitoring, so by default, everything got it.

Six practices that brought it back under control

1

Log level discipline

A caught exception that was handled correctly isn't an incident — log it at warning or info, not error. Every error-level log opens a full Sentry Issue, tracked and counted, whether or not anyone will ever act on it.

2

Never log inside a loop

One failure inside a loop can turn into hundreds of identical events. Log a summary before and after the operation instead of once per iteration.

3

Exclude exceptions you expect

If an exception is a known, handled case, add it to ignore_errors or catch it specifically — don't let Sentry re-discover the same "known issue" every day.

4

Gate new services before they ship

Decide on tracing intentionally, before merge, via traces_sampler — not by leaving the default on and revisiting it after the bill shows up.

5

Fix the N+1s, not just the alert

Every database call generates its own span. An N+1 query doesn't just slow down the request — it multiplies the tracing overhead billed for that single request.

6

Report retries strategically

Report an error once the retries are exhausted, not on every intermediate attempt. A request that eventually succeeds after two retries isn't three separate incidents.

What changed

The mental shift mattered more than any single fix: monitor what could plausibly need debugging, not everything that's technically traceable. Error capture and performance tracing are two different decisions, and treating log-level choice as a real code-review concern — not an afterthought — is what keeps both of them honest going forward.
